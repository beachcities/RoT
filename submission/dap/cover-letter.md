Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Return on Token: An Indicator Linking Data Self-Description to AI Inference Token Consumption," for publication in Data & Policy as a Research Article (standard track).

The article proposes Return on Token (RoT), an indicator of AI inference cost defined as outcome obtained per total tokens invested, with recursive and discarded search included in the denominator, and examines the hypothesis that RoT varies with the self-description of data. On a minimal open benchmark that varies self-description over ten levels, a boundary in consumption appeared at the same position in every system measured; the article reads the intermediate reasoning of one fully open system around that boundary, reports a first derivation of candidate data requirements from the reasoning of failed attempts, and develops — as explicitly conditional policy hypotheses — what such measurements could mean for prioritising investment in open data as shared information infrastructure. We believe this combination of a measurable indicator, an open and fully archived benchmark, and policy implications stated together with their evidential status fits the journal's scope at the intersection of data science and governance.
